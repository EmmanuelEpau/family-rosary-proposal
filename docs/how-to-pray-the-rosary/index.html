How to pray the Rosary

A step-by-step guide, with room to begin.

The Rosary combines familiar prayers with reflection on the life of Jesus and Mary. A usual five-decade Rosary includes an opening set of prayers, five mysteries with ten Hail Marys each, and closing prayers.

The stepsPray a decadeThe mysteriesYour questions

The steps, simply.

You don’t have to memorize everything first. Keep a guide nearby and pray at a pace that lets you reflect.

Open the ministry’s printable guides

Begin with the opening prayers

Make the Sign of the Cross and pray the Apostles’ Creed. Pray one Our Father, three Hail Marys, then the Glory Be.

Name the first mystery

Bring the mystery to mind and offer your intention. Pray an Our Father, ten Hail Marys and the Glory Be while reflecting. Many people add the Fatima Prayer.

Continue through five decades

Repeat the pattern for each of the remaining four mysteries. Each decade is ten Hail Marys, with an Our Father before and a Glory Be after.

Close in prayer

After five decades, pray the Hail, Holy Queen and the concluding prayer, then make the Sign of the Cross. The linked complete guide includes the prayer texts.

Pray one decade with us.

Choose a mystery. Offer a quiet intention. Then move through the prayers at your own pace.

This guided decade is one part of the Rosary. Use the steps above for the complete five-decade prayer.

The four sets of mysteries

The days below follow a common weekly pattern. Sunday customs can vary with the liturgical season; you may choose another set.

Joyful mysteries Monday and Saturday
  1. The Annunciation
  2. The Visitation
  3. The Nativity
  4. The Presentation
  5. The Finding of Jesus in the Temple
Sorrowful mysteries Tuesday and Friday
  1. The Agony in the Garden
  2. The Scourging at the Pillar
  3. The Crowning with Thorns
  4. The Carrying of the Cross
  5. The Crucifixion
Glorious mysteries Wednesday and Sunday
  1. The Resurrection

Luminous mysteries Thursday
  1. The Baptism of Jesus
  2. The Wedding at Cana
  3. The Proclamation of the Kingdom
  4. The Transfiguration
  5. The Institution of the Eucharist

Questions you might be bringing

Do I have to know every prayer?

No. Read the words from a guide as you go. You can learn the prayers through repetition.

Can I begin with one decade?

Yes. A decade gives you a manageable place to begin and learn the rhythm. It is part of the Rosary, not a replacement name for all five decades.

What if my children can’t sit still?

Give them a simple role, such as naming an intention or counting a decade. Try a short shared moment and build a routine that your family can grow into.

Where can I find a guide in another language?

The existing Family Rosary how-to page offers English, Spanish and Portuguese PDF guides. Open those guides
